The motivation behind Alex Schnell's campaign for Pennsylvania's 3rd District is straightforward: too many residents feel that their concerns never reach Washington. Decisions that shape neighborhoods are being made far from the people those neighborhoods belong to. Alex believes that has to change, and that it changes through a different kind of candidate — one who listens before legislating.

## The problem with how Washington works

> "Many residents feel that their concerns do not reach Washington. Too often, decisions are made far from the neighborhoods they affect."

This disconnect is not abstract. It shows up in policy choices that fail to reflect local realities, in representatives who are more responsive to party leadership than to constituents, and in a process that filters out candidates who haven't already earned the approval of the political class.

Alex's answer is not a policy platform alone — it is a different model of engagement. Representation, in his view, begins with consistent presence in the community and a genuine willingness to hear what residents are actually dealing with.

## Trust is built, not demanded

> "Trust in government cannot be demanded. It is built through transparency, accountability, and steady involvement."

Alex approaches the campaign the way he approaches his professional work: by making the process visible, inviting scrutiny, and showing up consistently. This site, including its source code, is publicly available for review. The signature-gathering process, the fundraising, and the platform positions are documented openly so that voters can evaluate the record rather than take anything on faith.

## A community-first focus

The goal is to run a campaign that reflects the district rather than one that asks the district to fall in line behind a preselected choice. Alex's commitment is to the people of PA-3 — not to party leadership, not to outside funders, and not to political organizations whose priorities may not align with the neighborhoods he seeks to represent.
