This campaign is built from the bottom up. Representation works best when it grows out of the community itself — when the people who live with federal decisions every day are the ones shaping the conversation. Every conversation and every connection strengthens the effort to build more responsive representation for PA-3.

## Open by design

The campaign website is hosted on GitHub, making every change publicly visible and traceable. The site is intentionally simple: HTML and CSS only, with no JavaScript frameworks and no third-party tracking. That choice reflects a deliberate commitment to speed, readability, and respect for visitor privacy.

Anyone can propose improvements or corrections to the site. Every suggestion is reviewed before it is accepted — nothing appears on the site without consideration and accountability. This community proposal model means that the campaign belongs to the community it represents, not just the candidate.

The review-before-merge process ensures that all contributions are evaluated for accuracy, tone, and alignment with the campaign's values before they become part of the public record.

## Why this matters

Transparency is not a slogan here — it is the structure. Hosting the campaign openly on GitHub means that every edit, every correction, and every content decision is part of a public audit trail. Accountability is built in at the architectural level, not bolted on afterward.

When a campaign is visible in this way, residents can see not just what is being said, but how decisions are made. That kind of institutional transparency creates a different relationship between a candidate and the community they seek to represent.
